235:| Columns, stairs, roofs | Columns of rectangular or round types, turned to any angle, from their level up to the next (or a height, or a level); cut in plan, in poché. Stairs from a start point toward an end point, in as many risers as keep each at most 180 mm, treads 280 mm deep, up to the next level: one straight run, or runs with a landing between each two (as deep as the stair is wide) after so many risers, the next run going on straight, turning left or right, or coming back beside the last; in plan, their risers up to the cut line and the rest dashed, landings over the cut plane dashed, and the walk line marked UP (DN from the level above). 243:| Reinforcement | `rebar` makes reinforcing bars: a round bar of a diameter (3 to 100 mm) and a steel grade (CA-50 unless told) bent along points in mm, in the beam, column, footing or floor it reinforces, which it goes with; `closed` makes a hoop, and `count` with `step` a layer of bars or hoops along a beam. A schedule of rebar lists grade, diameter, length, count and weight (steel at 7850 kg/m³) and, grouped, is a bar schedule. IFC writes and reads each as an `IfcReinforcingBar`, aggregated to its host. The 3D view draws them as tubes (inside their host, so hide it to see them), a section draws the bars it cuts as dots over the poché, and a plan draws each bar of its level as a thin line (solid under the cut plane, dashed over it and under the view range's top, a bar that stands as a dot, bars lying on one another drawn once), hidden with the category. `reinforce` arms a beam, a column, a footing or a floor (round columns and footings, and sloped floors, too) by rules: the engineer gives the cover (or the host's `Cobrimento` parameter) and how many bars, how thick and how far apart (a beam's bars below and above, stirrups with closer ends and hooks, skin bars and anchors; a column's bars on each face, stirrups and ties; a footing's two layers of mesh with their ends turned up; a floor's meshes below and above, stopping short of its outline and openings), and it lays them out inside the cover, checks they fit with room between them and says what to change; a beam's bars and a column's end in hooks if told, some of a beam's bottom bars bend up at each end to the top at an angle (`bent`) and a column's bars step in toward its axis near the top (`jog`); each bar is on a layer, and reinforcing again replaces what it made and leaves bars drawn by hand. `hosts` arms a list alike, all or none (up to 1000 hosts and 20000 bars), and `reinforcement_preset` keeps a named set of the parameters for a kind of host in the project, which `reinforce` takes with `preset` and lets what is given replace. The numbers are the engineer's; `check` "NBR 6118" (with the aggressiveness class and `fck`) adds notes, never a refusal, where the bars made fall short of its usual limits (cover, bar sizes and spacings, stirrups, least and most steel, skin bars), typed from public handbooks and not from the standard's text, so check one against a copy before relying on it. On the desktop, the Structure tab's Reinforce (RF) opens a dialog for the elements selected (any number, of one kind), with the fields of their kind, saying a refusal inside itself, keeping what was typed for the next of its kind, saving the fields as a preset to choose for another and deleting one, and with Remove Bars for the bars it made. Not yet: bends other than a hook, a bent-up bar and a column's jog, round beams, bars beyond a section's cut seen through the concrete. |
